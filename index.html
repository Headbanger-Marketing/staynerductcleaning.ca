Settling duct and vent scope with co-owners before anyone is booked

The rooms, vents and equipment involved go into any request for a Stayner home. With several owners, though, duct or vent work can stall because each person pictures a different job. One may want the dryer vent looked at, another the registers, a third nothing at all. List what each owner has noticed, who must agree before a provider is chosen, and who will speak for the household. That lets whichever provider responds quote one scope instead of several versions.

  1. Return grilles

    Openings that pull room air back into the system.

  2. Return ducts

    The runs leading from each grille to the furnace.

  3. Furnace blower and coil

    The blower, evaporator coil and cabinet.

  4. Supply trunk

    The largest duct carrying air away from the furnace.

  5. Branch runs

    The individual runs that split off toward each room.

  6. Supply registers

    The floor, wall or ceiling vents in each room.

Separate runs to ask about:Dryer ventHRV core and screensBathroom fan ductsRange hood exhaust

Questions About Your Stayner Request

Is Stayner Duct Cleaning a duct cleaning contractor I can hire?

Stayner Duct Cleaning is an enquiry website, not a contractor, and nothing on it is a provider's offer. Our enquiry team reviews each request and may pass it to a responding provider. That provider must identify themselves, confirm that they cover your address and put the scope, pricing and timing in writing before any work is arranged.

What happens next with a duct or vent request from my Stayner home?

Sending your request does not arrange a provider, fix a price or confirm coverage, and it obliges no owner to proceed. Instead, it passes your description to our enquiry team for review. If a provider responds, every owner can still compare the proposed scope and decline it. Nothing is arranged until the people who own the home agree and the details are in writing.

Who needs to agree before duct work is arranged in a Stayner home with several owners?

That depends on how the home is owned, and this site gives no legal advice. Ask the other owners, or a real estate representative or lawyer if the question is unclear, who must approve spending and who may sign. Then name that person as the contact in your request, so the provider knows whose written approval counts.

How do I pass each owner's questions to the provider through one contact?

Collect every owner's questions in one list, grouped by service, and add them to the description with the contact's name. The provider's answers then go back through that one person, who shares them with the others. This keeps owners from receiving different versions of the same quote, and it leaves a single written trail of who asked what.

What if the owners disagree about whether the dryer vent is included in a Stayner request?

Send the request with the dryer vent listed as a separate line, marked as undecided. A dryer exhaust is its own run, so the provider can respond to the ducts and the vent separately. The owners can then accept one line and decline the other without restarting the inquiry or losing the rest of the written scope.

Can one owner authorize duct work on behalf of the others?

Only if the others have clearly agreed, and that agreement is not something this site can confirm. Ask the provider to state in the written scope who approved the work, and ask the other owners to confirm in writing, such as a short message, that the named contact may sign. Questions about ownership rights belong with a lawyer, not with this site or the provider.

Which photos and details should every owner see before my Stayner request is sent?

Share the register and return grille photos, the labels on any furnace, air handler or HRV, and a picture of the dryer vent hood outside. Add a short note of anything each owner has noticed, such as dust, a musty smell or a room with weak airflow. When everyone has seen the same material, the inquiry reflects the whole household and not one person's view.

What happens if a provider finds extra work after the owners approved a scope?

The provider should stop at the agreed scope, describe the extra item and its location, and wait for approval. The contact owner should pass that note to everyone before answering. An item outside the agreement, such as a damaged section or an added treatment, is a separate decision and is written down as one, with its own price and approval.
